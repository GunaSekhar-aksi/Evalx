# MSBEH — Behavioral, Projects, AA (As-Appropriate) round (Microsoft)

> Weightage: ~15%, and it can veto everything else. In aggregated reports, behavioral/motivation
> shows up MORE often than CS fundamentals. The final "AA" round is usually a senior person judging
> whether they'd want you on the team. Deep project questions are standard.

## Microsoft's lens: growth mindset
Microsoft's culture talk centers on **growth mindset**: learning from failure, being curious, collaborating. Every story you tell should show you *learned and changed*, not that you were always right.

## Answer format: STAR (keep it to about 2 minutes)
**S**ituation (1 line) -> **T**ask (1 line) -> **A**ction (most of the time, and say "I," not "we") -> **R**esult (with a number if possible) -> **what you'd do differently** (the growth-mindset part most people skip).

## 1. Tell me about yourself
60–90 seconds: who you are -> the one or two strongest things you've built -> why that leads to this role. Don't recite your resume line by line; they already have it.

## 2. Why Microsoft?
Be specific: a product or team you actually use or care about (Azure, VS Code, GitHub, the developer tooling ecosystem), plus the scale of impact, plus the learning culture. "Big company, good pay" is the answer that gets you remembered for the wrong reasons.

## 3. Deep dive into your project (always asked)
Expect: Why this tech and not the alternative? What was the hardest bug? How does it scale? What breaks first under 10x load? What would you redesign?
**Rule:** If it's on your resume, you must be able to defend every word. Anything you can't explain, remove it.

## 4. A time you failed / made a mistake
Pick a real failure that you owned. Spend most of the time on what you changed afterward. "I worked too hard" is not a failure; it's a cliché.

## 5. Conflict with a teammate
Show that you listened first, disagreed using data instead of ego, and committed once the decision was made ("disagree and commit"). Never make the other person the villain.

## 6. Tight deadline / time management (reported)
How you prioritized (what you cut, and why), how you communicated the risk *early*, and the result.

## 7. Learning something new fast
A concrete example: the technology, how you learned it, and what you shipped using it, with a timeline.

## 8. Going beyond your scope
Something you did that nobody asked for, and how it helped the team or the users.

## 9. A recent tech update you found interesting (reported)
Pick something you actually understand well enough to handle two follow-up questions. Prepare one now so you're not making it up on the spot.

## 10. Questions for them (always have 2)
"What does a strong first 6 months look like on this team?" / "What's the hardest technical problem the team is facing right now?"
Never ask about salary or leave in a technical round.

## Prep: write 6 stories that cover everything
Failure, conflict, leadership/ownership, tight deadline, learning fast, the project you're proudest of. Each story can be reused for 3–4 different questions.
